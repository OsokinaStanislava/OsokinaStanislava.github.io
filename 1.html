ABOUT

What is "HoMM"?
Heroes of Might and Magic is a series of video games originally created and developed by New World Computing as part of the Might and Magic franchise. Think of it as the ultimate "just one more turn" simulator. It's a beautiful, chaotic fusion of genres that shouldn't work but absolutely does.

Why is it so popular?
Heroes of Might and Magic is popular because it's the ultimate fantasy life-sim. For one glorious session, you're not just playing a strategy game; you're a powerful lord, exploring a mysterious land, building a kingdom, and leading your fantastical armies to glory. It's a perfect storm of exploration, empire-building, character progression, and tactical combat. It gives you the freedom to be the protagonist of your own high-fantasy epic, where you are equally the master strategist, the intrepid explorer, and the powerful archmage, all wrapped in a package that feels like a beloved, slightly-whacky board game night with your best friends. It’s not just a game. It’s a world you get to live inside for a few hours.

What's the story of development?
Before there were Heroes, there was just Might and Magic. This was a series of classic, first-person, party-based RPGs from a small American company called New World Computing, founded by the visionary Jon Van Caneghem. They were huge, ambitious, and very successful in their own right.
In 1990, New World Computing made a weird little spin-off. It wasn't a first-person RPG. It was a strategic, turn-based adventure game called King's Bounty. You controlled a single hero on a map, recruited armies from castles, and hunted for a hidden treasure. It was the direct, undeniable prototype for Heroes of Might and Magic. It had the core loop: hero, army, map, turn-based combat. But it was a single, contained quest.
The genius of JVC and his team was seeing the potential. They took the King's Bounty framework and injected it with pure, uncut RPG and empire-building steroids. The first Heroes of Might and Magic was born. It was a hit. It was like someone took the addictive gameplay of King's Bounty and built an entire universe around it.
This is where the legend was forged, and also where the corporate shadow falls. New World Computing was acquired by a struggling company called The 3DO Company, best known for... their failed console. Paradoxically, this corporate ownership gave the developers the resources and freedom to create their masterpiece. They released HoMM III. It wasn't just a sequel; it was a cultural artifact. It was the pinnacle of 2D isometric art, with an insane amount of content, near-perfect balance, and a depth that has never been truly replicated. But 3DO, as a company, was bleeding money from their other failures. They milked the Might and Magic IP dry, forcing rushed sequels (like HoMM IV, which was ambitious but deeply flawed) and cheap spin-offs. The quality plummeted. In 2003, 3DO went bankrupt. The rights to the entire Might and Magic IP were sold in pieces. The big one, Heroes of Might and Magic, was bought by... Ubisoft.
HoMM V, VI, VII... a series of sometimes-good, sometimes-disappointing sequels trying to recapture the glory. Meanwhile... The original creators of the spirit of the game weren't idle. The developers of the original King's Bounty went on to create the King's Bounty reboot series (like The Legend), which is essentially "Heroes of Might and Magic, but with a single, RPG-focused hero." It's a direct lineage back to the 1990 original.
It's a story of brilliant innovation, a golden age under a strange corporate patron, a catastrophic collapse, and a franchise forever split between its official owners and its heart, which still beats strongest in a 25-year-old masterpiece.
